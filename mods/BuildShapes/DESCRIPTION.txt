Draw curves, loops, circles, arches and straight runs of beams or walls, rows of posts or decorations with gaps, and mirror pieces as shared BuildOrders ghosts. Requires BuildOrders with its planning and ghost-selection APIs. Hallwright (timber halls, longhouses, stave temples) is its own mod since BuildShapes 0.9.0: install Hallwright for the stake.

Pick a piece in the hammer as usual, then hold Left Ctrl (about a third of a second, without clicking or scrolling) for the shape radial: flick towards Curve, Arch, Circle or Mirror and let go (or click). It looks like the game's own radial; it shows only the shapes that work with the piece in your hand, and Build leaves the shape tool. Escape, or letting go in the middle, closes it.

In a shape tool: left click places markers; once they're all down, the shape shows as a sketch (below). Scroll rolls them about their length and Shift+scroll turns each about its own up (in the game's 22.5° steps), E plans them as shared ghosts (and uses nothing you're looking at), Delete clears the shape and keeps the tool, Backspace removes the last marker, U removes your last shape's unbuilt ghosts, and Escape leaves the tool. Ctrl+scroll sets how round a Curve is and an Arch's height; Ctrl+Shift+scroll sets the gap between pieces (none, or up to 16 m, shrunk evenly so the outer pieces still meet the ends: a row of posts, torches or banners along the curve, as Repeat used to do). Ctrl+click selects pieces in Mirror; a slow Ctrl+click or Ctrl+scroll that opens the radial still does what it would have done.

Curve: pick any piece with a length (a beam, pole, stone block, wall or pillar) and click points: two make a straight line (at any slope, or upright), a third and more a curve through them all in the order you clicked (up to 64). Click the first point again to close the loop; it becomes just another point on the curve. E plans at any point, Backspace removes the last point (or opens the loop), Delete starts over. Ctrl+scroll sets how round it runs through its points, 100% down to 0%: at 0% the sides are straight with sharp corners and pieces end exactly at each corner. Home switches how pieces follow it: along the curve in 3D (beams, pillars) or upright, standing plumb and turning to follow it, by the bottom of their ends, at the lower end's height on a slope (walls, fences); a pole or pillar stands side by side instead, upright, spaced by its width along the curve (a palisade). Walls, poles and pillars start standing; Home lays them along the curve (stacked poles for a tall post). Pieces are laid end to end along their own length: the axis their snap points spread furthest along (a stone block lies along the path; a pillar along its height), or their mesh's longest side; 0.1–8 metres long, native length, overlaps spread evenly. Two points replace the old Line tool.

Arch: pick a piece with a length and click both ends: the circle through both ends and its top, its ends leaning as the circle has them (a half-circle stands up at its ends). Ctrl+scroll sets the height above the ends' middle (0–32 m); a gold guide marks the top. Unequal end heights work; the ends must be apart horizontally. (It was Arc before 0.13.0.)

Circle: click the centre, then a point on the edge. The circle lies level at the edge point's height and its pieces go evenly all the way round; Home, roll, turn and gaps work as in Curve.

Mirror: click two points for a vertical mirror plane, then Ctrl+click built pieces or visible ghosts to toggle source selection. Thin wire boxes show sources; the mirrored copies show as real pieces. Ctrl+Backspace removes the last selection. Reflection axes and pivot compensation follow native snap layouts, fixing triangular under-roof walls and sloped beams while preserving ordinary roof pitch; mesh bounds are a fallback. Asymmetric carvings and handed meshes keep their original geometry, so inspect the ghosts before building.

Live draft sharing: other players with BuildShapes see your markers and shape previews as cyan visuals labeled with your name. Sharing is on by default; F7 exposes send/receive and piece-preview toggles under Shared drafts. These visuals have no colliders, costs or build actions; E still creates normal shared BuildOrders ghosts. Drafts clear on submission/exit/unload and expire after disconnects. Viewers draw the nearest four drafts within 120 m, with at most 4,096 shared pieces visible.

Sketch preview (the default): the shape is drawn like an architect's watercolour, as Hallwright drafts are: pen strokes along every edge where a piece folds or ends, over a wash of the piece's own colour on watercolour-paper grain. It draws itself in along the shape, ink first, when it's first complete; turning or reshaping it afterwards shows at once. PreviewStyle (F7, General) = Pieces shows the real pieces instead. Either way previews are local only (no colliders, no cost). While a shape tool is out, its keys stand in for the game's key hints along the bottom of the screen, in the same look, with a line above for the shape, its markers and its settings. Curves and circles are limited to 256 metres round, Arch to 128, and every shape to 256 pieces. Markers/sources require 40-metre reach; the planner checks all output positions, recipes, wards, and no-build areas. Scaled and terrain-operation pieces are excluded. Selections are pose snapshots.

Shapes never change terrain; built pieces stay intact and normal materials/support still apply. Undo is one submitted shape across all tools and clears on death, world change, or F6. Only the designer needs BuildShapes; participating players should use updated BuildOrders and the selected pieces. Keys are in F7 (Controls): ShapeMenu (Left Ctrl), ConfirmShape (E). ClaudeTools: shapes radial [wedge|off] holds the radial open, and shapes hints <tool|off> [full] shows a tool's key hints, for pictures.
