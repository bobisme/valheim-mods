Homespun cloth, from the field to the back. Flax, nettle and wool go through the steps they really went through, at stations you build at a workbench (Crafting tab), and come out as cloth and clothes.

Nettle: the cloth plant you have from the start, before flax. It grows wild in meadow clearings and along the Black Forest's edge, and grows back after you pick it; greydwarfs sometimes carry a bundle. Sow a nettle with the cultivator (Meadows or Black Forest) and it grows into a stand that gives 2 or 3. Retted and broken as flax is, its shorter, coarser fibre spins into half the thread: a line nettle makes 1 linen thread to a line flax's 2. Wild nettle only appears in ground you haven't explored yet.

Retting pool: a plank trough of water. Put flax or nettle in and it soaks for 45 seconds a bundle, the stalks rotting loose from the fibre: retted flax or nettle. Holds 40. 16 wood, 6 stone, 4 resin.

Flax brake: a hinged wooden jaw that breaks the retted stalks, with a hackle of iron teeth that combs the fibre straight: line flax or line nettle, 10 seconds each. It combs a lox pelt into 4 wool, too. Holds 20. 12 wood, 10 iron nails.

Spinning: the game's spinning wheel spins line flax into 2 linen thread (raw flax still gives 1, as it always has), line nettle into 1, and wool into yarn. Or carry a drop spindle (workbench: 2 wood, 1 stone) and spin as you walk: hold it and it spins what's in your bag, line flax first, then line nettle, then wool, a thread or yarn every minute or so.

Warp-weighted loom: a frame leaned back, the warp hanging from it on stone weights. Weave a linen bolt (8 linen thread), a wool bolt (8 yarn) or 2 woven bands (2 thread, 2 yarn). 20 wood, 10 stone, 2 leather scraps.

Sewing table: cut and sew bolts into clothes. Linen undertunic, wool tunic, wool dress, wool trousers and a hooded wool cloak, in the colours of undyed linen and wool: light, warm work clothes, not armour. And Hildir's coloured clothes, made here from cloth (berries for the colour) instead of bought. 12 wood, 2 leather scraps, 1 bronze.

With Byre installed, its sheep's fleece spins straight into yarn, no lox pelt needed.

Dye vat: a stave tub. Open it as you would a chest, put in bolts of cloth or clothes and one dyestuff, and a minute later they come out dyed: blueberries dye blue, raspberries red, dandelions yellow, and lichen purple. A bolt takes a measure of dye (5 berries or dandelions, 3 lichen), a garment the bolts it was sewn from. Dyed bolts sew into garments of their colour at the sewing table, and a garment can be dyed again in another colour. Its wear and mending go with it. Dyed things are items of their own, so their colour shows worn, dropped and in a chest, for every player. 12 wood, 3 stone, 2 resin.

Tapestries: what the dyed cloth is for. A procession tapestry (a long band as in the Oseberg ship: horses, a wagon and spearmen; a linen bolt and a red, a blue and a yellow wool bolt) and a wall hanging (as at Överhogdal: elk, a ship, birds and a tree in rows; two linen bolts, a red and a blue wool bolt), in the hammer's Furniture tab, hung on a wall as the game's banners are. Comfort 2, in the banners' group.

Lichen: crusted on stones in the Mountains and the Black Forest (newly explored ground only, as nettle). Pick it and the stone stays; the lichen grows back slowly.

Removing Homespun: as with any mod that adds things, its items and pieces are gone for good once a character or a place is loaded without it (the game drops what it doesn't know from bags, chests and the ground, and takes down the pieces). Line flax, line nettle or wool waiting in a spinning wheel is spun to nothing. Nettle growing in the world, wild or sown, and the lichen stones are gone too; so is everything dyed, and whatever is in a dye vat. Hildir's clothes you sewed are the game's own and stay.
