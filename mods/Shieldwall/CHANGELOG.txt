0.3.5: Staves only fire at what they can see. Tree crowns now block their sight as they do a monster's (they didn't, so a stave would fire at a neck deep in the woods), anything solid anywhere on the line blocks it (not just the first thing it meets), and a stave won't lead its shot into a hill. The horde's road line is now a setting, ShowRoad in F7, off by default; the rift's beacon and map pin still show where they come from. Claude Tools: siege sight lists what stands between each stave and every foe in reach.

0.3.4: A stave socket can only be built within the Warstone's reach and while the stone has power to spare for it (2 sockets at level 1, 2 more each level); the hammer says why when it refuses. A fed stave glows in its colour with a pulsing light; a sleeping one (beyond the stone's reach or its count, say after an upgrade is torn down) stands dark as cold iron with no light.

0.3.3: Fixed building the Warstone by hand: it showed no ghost, took its cost and stood nowhere to be seen. The game sets a ghost down by its convex colliders and the stone had none, so the ghost (and the stone) went to about twice your coordinates, 50 m up. The stone's collider is now convex. Warstones built with 0.3.2 or older stand far off at about twice the coordinates where you aimed them.

0.3.2: Fixed a Warstone vanishing (and its cost lost) when built in someone else's game. A host's game deletes any object whose prefab it does not know, so a host without Shieldwall deleted the stone as soon as it loaded it. Your game now asks the host whether it has Shieldwall 0.3.2 or newer; if not, the Warstone, sockets and upgrades refuse to be built and nothing is spent. A dedicated server without the mod still hosts sieges, except within 400 m of the middle of the world, where it keeps the land loaded and deletes unknown objects too.

0.3.1: Dundr (and any staff with hidden parts) stands centred in its socket: the look of a set staff no longer includes parts the game keeps switched off (Dundr's hidden 'loaded' glow), which pulled it to one side, and a staff's shaft is found from the bottom third of its height.

0.3.0: Sockets open like a chest: Use shows the socket's one slot beside your bag, so you choose which stave stands there (no more auto-pick), and the game's own container keeps it, saves it and drops it if the socket falls. Staves stand straight and centred in their pedestals, all about four metres tall: each staff's shaft is found from its own mesh and its butt measured once by eye (a tiny render). The Thunder stave is now built on the Trollstav, a cleaner straight staff. Moats: diggers (from the second wave) raise ramps of earth over moats and cliffs with the game's own terrain tools, any raider stuck behind bare land long enough starts one, and diggers that reach the stone dig at its foot. A stone whose land sinks a metre below where it was set topples into the pit: it cracks, loses a mark, and a siege on is lost. Sockets built with 0.2 need a restart to get their slot.

0.2.1: Fixed a stave vanishing when set in a socket. A stave picked up after a hot reload could carry a dead link to its item type; the socket took it from your bag and stored nothing. Sockets now know a stave by its name and refuse rather than take an item they cannot keep, and a reload re-links Shieldwall's items lying on the ground as well as in your bag.

0.2.0: Towers. War staves are now huge relics too heavy to wield, set in stave sockets: stone pedestals built on the ground, walls or tower tops, 5 m apart. Use a socket to set or take out a stave; Shift+Use strengthens it in place with warshards. The Warstone has a level (1-5) raised by four upgrade pieces built beside it (war banner, trophy totem, horned brazier, rune obelisk; the horde barely scratches them); it feeds 2-10 staves within 26-50 m, nearest first, and the level gates stave recipes and strengthening. Sappers go for sockets and tear down the tower under one that stands high; a raider a stave keeps hitting may turn on its tower; warchiefs strike buildings half again as hard. Staves aim at whoever is closest to the stone (Thunder at the strongest) and count their kills. Shift+E on the stone calls the next wave early for bonus warshards, and its hover names what the next wave holds. The siege bar is now the game's own boss bar. Staves planted in the ground by 0.1 fall over as items, ready for a socket.

0.1.2: Quicker sieges: waves come at most 40 s apart (was 75; existing settings keep their value), and one or two raiders stuck out of sight end a siege after 75 s instead of 150. The spoils chest appears 4.5 m from the stone on the side away from the rift, out of the next horde's swing. A clearer warshard icon. More Claude Tools checks: sound the horn, plant what you hold, open the stone's menu, and fire a base raid at the stone to see it turn into a siege. Live-tested: the horn, raids drawn to the stone, recipe discovery from a warshard, 60 fps through a siege.

0.1.1: A held siege now ends with a summary (how many fell, how the stone stood, its new rank, how many warshards it left) and a burst of light at the stone. The Hearth stave mends a besieged stone by half a percent of its strength each pulse (more when upgraded) instead of a token 30. Test helpers for Claude Tools: start a chosen horde, place a stone at coordinates, remove one.

0.1.0: First release. The Warstone (ward, crafting station, war horn), sieges in waves from a rift with a shown road and breach point, seven hordes by world progress with sappers, flyers and warchiefs, spoils and marks, cracking, five plantable staves with upgrades, raids drawn to the stone, a siege bar and raid music.
