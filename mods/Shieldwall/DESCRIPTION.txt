A game within the game: build a Warstone, raise towers with war staves around it, and hold the line when a horde comes for it.

The Warstone (hammer, misc; 30 stone, 10 wood, 5 flint, 5 resin, near a workbench) is a standing stone that wards your home. Everyone within 40 metres gets faster health and stamina regeneration, and with each siege the stone holds (its marks), carry weight, eitr regeneration and armor. Set it under a roof with a fire burning within 10 metres and the ward is twice as strong and adds comfort. One Warstone to a home.

Raise the stone's level (1 to 5) by building its upgrades within 10 metres: a war banner, a trophy totem, a horned brazier and a rune obelisk, each paid in warshards. The level decides how many war staves the stone can feed (2 to 10), how far (26 to 50 metres), which staves it can craft and how strong they can be made.

War staves are huge relics, too heavy to wield. Set them in stave sockets, stone pedestals you build on the ground, on walls or on top of towers, at least 5 metres apart. Use a socket to open its slot and choose the stave (the game's own chest window), Shift+Use to strengthen it in place with warshards. Staves fire on raiders and monsters in reach, aim at whoever is closest to the stone, count their kills, and never hurt players, companions, tames or buildings:
Ember stave (level 1): fireballs that splash.
Frost stave (level 2): a fast stream of ice shards that chill.
Hearth stave (level 2): mends you, your companions, your tames and, in a siege, the stone.
Thunder stave (level 3): heavy bolts at the strongest foe in 42 metres.
Blast stave (level 4): bombs that burst over the crowd.
Any other staff that throws something can be set in a socket too, weakly.

Shift+E on the stone sounds its war horn. A rift opens 60 to 95 metres away and a horde gathers, the strongest your world has unlocked: greydwarfs at first, then the Black Forest's host, the drowned dead, a mountain pack, a Fuling warband, the swarm of the mist and the Charred legion. After 90 seconds it marches on the stone in four to eight waves, ending with a warchief. A glowing line shows the road it will take and bends as you build: walls turn it aside, and where the road is shut a red line marks where they will break through. They fight what stands close, smash what blocks the way, and strike the stone when they reach it. Sappers go for your towers and tear down what holds them up, a raider a stave keeps hitting may turn on its tower, warchiefs strike buildings half again as hard, bats, drakes, deathsquitos and gjall fly over the walls, and diggers raise ramps over moats and dig at the stone's foot: undermine a Warstone (or dig your moat too close) and it topples, cracks and loses a mark. The game's own boss bar counts the waves and the stone's strength, with the game's raid music; the stone names what the next wave holds, and its horn can call the next wave early for bonus warshards.

Hold until the last of them falls and spoils appear by the stone: a chest of that land with warshards, coins and materials. Each siege held adds a mark: the ward grows and the horde grows. If they break the stone it cracks: its ward weakens until it holds a siege again. Nothing is lost; a fallen tower's stave lies where it stood. The first siege is fought by hand: the horde's warshards teach you the staves.

Once you have a Warstone, a base raid that would hit near it becomes a siege at the stone instead, with three minutes' warning (needs Shieldwall on the host).

Sieges run on the game of the player nearest the stone, so they work on a dedicated server without the mod; everyone who joins in needs Shieldwall. Warnings, wave pacing, the most raiders at once, the time limit and the horn's cooldown are in F7.
