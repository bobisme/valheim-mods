Local Portals: tall wooden mirrors you can see through and walk through, with no loading screen. Built with the hammer next to the game's portal (workbench nearby; 10 fine wood, 6 greydwarf eyes, 1 surtling core).

Build two within 80 m of each other and the second links to the first; one glows blue, the other orange. Or name them like the game's portals (look at one and press E): two mirrors with the same name are linked, and a name links just two, so a base can have as many pairs as it likes. The mirror tells you what it is linked to, or why it is not.

The glass shows the other side as it really is from where you stand, live, from any angle and distance, with the world's light and fog. Walk into it and you step out of the other mirror, moving and looking the same way, carrying your speed (and your fall). The camera follows you through: for a moment it looks through the first mirror at you coming out of the second, and if the second mirror is in its way from behind, it is cut away. While you are partway through, the part of you that has gone in is seen coming out of the other mirror, on every player's screen.

The mirrors are solid from behind and at the sides; only their glass lets you through, from the front. Only players go through, not items, creatures or carts (you cannot go through pulling a cart). Both ends must be loaded, which 80 m keeps true.

Each live view draws the world again: about 0.7 ms a view at 4K in testing. Settings: how many mirrors show a live view at once (2), their sharpness, and the body doubles.

Everyone in a multiplayer game needs the mod: a host without it deletes the mirrors when their area loads. Removing the mod removes built mirrors.
