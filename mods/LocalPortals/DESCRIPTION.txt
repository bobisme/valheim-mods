Looking-Glass Portals: tall, root-bound looking-glasses you can see through and walk through, with no loading screen. An old root climbs one side of each frame and curls over the arch, runes glow up the other side in the mirror's colour, and a crystal circled by shards floats above. Built with the hammer next to the game's portal (workbench nearby; 10 fine wood, 6 greydwarf eyes, 1 surtling core).

Build two within 200 m of each other (a setting, up to 2 km) and the second links to the first; one glows blue, the other orange. Or name them like the game's portals (look at one and press E): two mirrors with the same name are linked, and a name links just two, so a base can have as many pairs as it likes. The mirror tells you what it is linked to, or why it is not.

The glass shows the other side as it really is from where you stand, live, from any angle and distance, with the world's light and fog. Walk into it and you step out of the other mirror, moving and looking the same way, carrying your speed (and your fall). The camera follows you through: for a moment it looks through the first mirror at you coming out of the second, and if the second mirror is in its way from behind, it turns to a faint glow you see through. While you are partway through, the part of you that has gone in is seen coming out of the other mirror, on every player's screen, and nothing of you shows behind either one.

Shoot an arrow, throw a spear or a bomb, or drop something into the glass and it comes out of the other mirror the same way, and flies on. The mirrors are solid from behind and at the sides; only their glass lets you through, from the front. Creatures that fit the opening walk through too: your followers come after you, and whatever hunts you follows you in. Trolls, deer and fliers are too big or never touch the ground, and carts do not go through (you cannot go through pulling one).

Each live view draws the world again: about 0.7 ms a view at 4K in testing. Settings: how far apart mirrors link (200 m; the host's in multiplayer), whether the area round a far partner is all loaded as you approach, how many mirrors show a live view at once (2), their sharpness, and the body doubles.

While you are near a mirror, the area round its partner is kept loaded and alive (pieces out there cannot fall), so the far side is a living world and you step out into ground that is ready. That works for the host or a single player; a player who joined someone else's game sees far mirrors only when both ends are near them.

Everyone in a multiplayer game needs the mod: a host without it deletes the mirrors when their area loads. Removing the mod removes built mirrors.
