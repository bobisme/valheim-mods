0.3.3: Less network traffic near mirrors in multiplayer. When a mirror's far side lay just past the area the host keeps alive, the game let go of everything out there every two seconds and the mod took it straight back, resending every object in those zones to every player each time; now it simply isn't let go.

0.3.2: The board behind the glass no longer shows streaks fanning out from one point (it is made of strips across now). Built mirrors take the fix on their own.
0.3.1: The hum is half as loud, and stepping through is silent again (no rush of air). A mirror waking chimes instead.
0.3.0: The mirrors have a voice: a low hum from a linked mirror, a rush of air as you step through, now and then a chime from the crystals, and the far side heard through the glass (sounds made near the other mirror, and the air of another kind of land: birds of a far meadow, wind off a mountain). Light from the far side falls out of the glass, coloured and as bright as what the view shows. A mirror seen in another mirror's view shows its own view, using views left over from MaxViews (never more). More mirrors in sight than MaxViews take turns, so all of them show their view instead of a plain film. Far mirrors work for players who joined: the host keeps their far side sent to them and moving for them (needs the host on 0.3.0). Optional, off at first: mirrors on the map with a line between each pair (Map/ShowMirrors), and the game's portal rule for ore, metal and eggs (Linking/BlockUnportableItems, the host's). For other mods: LookingGlassApi places mirrors, locks their names, and puts them to sleep until offered an item or until a boss is slain.
0.2.2: A wild looking-glass for other mods to place (prefab BobWildLookingGlass): rough split rock run into the ground, roots hanging over it, glowing crystals grown out of the stone, boulders round its feet. Not built and not in the hammer; it cannot be damaged or taken down, and links only by name. Mirrors that are not wild never link to it on their own.
0.2.1: The hammer's build menu shows the looking-glass itself (drawn from its own model as the game starts: root, runes, glowing blue glass and floating crystal) instead of the game's portal.
0.2.0: Now called Looking-Glass Portals (they reach far beyond local now). A new look: an old root grows up one side of the frame, winds round it and curls over the arch; runes glow up the other side, crystals grow by the foot, and a crystal circled by shards floats above the arch in place of the surtling core. The glowing inlay shows at last (it had been cut away as see-through). Built mirrors take the new look; their names, links and settings carry over.
0.1.9: Settings for far mirrors. Linking/MaxDistance (200 m by default, 20 to 2000) is the host's in multiplayer: players joining play by the host's value. Linking/PreloadOnApproach (on) loads all of the area round a far partner when you are within 25 m, for a smooth step through; off loads less, with a short hitch on arriving. Finding mirrors no longer costs more the farther they link. Tested working at 1 km.
0.1.8: Mirrors link up to 200 m apart (was 80). While you are near a mirror the area round its partner is kept loaded and alive, however far it is, so its view shows a living world, things there move, and stepping through lands you in an area that is already loaded (within 25 m of a mirror, all of it). Linking works from the world's saved data, so a partner too far off to be loaded is still found. Far mirrors work for the host or a single player; a player joining someone else's game still needs both ends near them.
0.1.7: Creatures walk through the mirrors. Anything that fits the opening (greydwarves, boars, wolves, necks; not trolls, deer or fliers) goes through like a player, and creatures take a mirror when it is much the shorter way: followers come after you, and whatever is hunting you knows which mirror you went through and follows. The part of a creature partway through is seen coming out of the other mirror.
0.1.6: Arrows, thrown spears, bombs and dropped items go through the mirrors: whatever flies into the glass comes out of the other mirror at the same spot, moving the same way, and flies on (a shot near the edge still hits the frame). A shot loosed while your bow is already past the glass comes out of the other mirror too. Claude Tools can reload the mod in a world.
0.1.5: A mirror in the camera's way fades smoothly between solid wood and its see-through glow (about a third of a second each way) instead of switching at once.
0.1.4: A mirror between the camera and you no longer vanishes: its wood turns to a faint see-through glow in its colour, with its inlay, gem and motes as they were, so you see yourself through it and can still tell it is there.
0.1.3: Walking into a mirror against a wall works: low wall pieces behind it (from a hand above the floor) let you through too, and your shoulders can pass the edges of the frame while you are lined up with the opening (you are nearly as wide as it, so being a little off centre used to stop you).
0.1.2: A mirror can stand right against a wall: walls just behind its glass no longer stop you before you are through (you had to get your middle past the glass, and the wall held you back). Floors and the ground still hold you up.
0.1.1: Nothing of you pokes out of the back of either mirror while you are partway through: your body is cut off at the glass you are entering, and the double coming out of the other mirror is cut off at its glass, so from behind both mirrors show only their wooden backs.
0.1.0: First Local Portals. A hammer piece next to the game's portal (10 fine wood, 6 greydwarf eyes, 1 surtling core, at a workbench): a tall arched mirror of dark wood whose glass shows a live view out of its linked mirror and which you walk straight through, with no loading screen. Two built within 80 m link (blue and orange), or two given the same name with E. The camera follows you through the glass, a mirror in its way from behind is cut away, and the part of you that has gone through is seen coming out of the other mirror. Views draw the game's fog and are only as big as the glass on screen; settings for how many live views, their sharpness and the body doubles.
