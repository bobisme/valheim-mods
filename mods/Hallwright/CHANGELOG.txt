0.12.5: A stone keep's walls go on down into the ground, bonded the same, instead of standing on a slab set in from them; inside, its timber floor is on stone pillars and beams like the other styles.

0.12.4: Stone keep: the stone is laid like brickwork, each course's joints a metre along from the course below's, with 1 m blocks to finish a run. Its entrance is a double gate (two gates side by side, 4 m wide) under a round arch of two stone arch pieces; the keep's storeys default to 4 m so the arch fits over the 3 m gate (4 m storeys need a 12 m stair run inside). The gate needs 5 m of straight wall around it.

0.12.3: A mountain cabin's gable logs go halfway up the gable, and every course reaches the roof on both sides (its logs overlap to fit); the boards behind them are gone, and only the upper half of the gable is boarded.

0.12.2: Mountain cabin, as a log cabin is built: every log course runs half a metre past the corners, crossing the other wall's; logs fill the gables, each course resting on the one below and as long as fits under the roof; the chimney is a crib of stacked logs; the footing is two courses of fieldstone fence with a dressed stone filling each outside corner. Stone keep corners are filled the same way, every course. Under timber halls, temples and cabins the stone foundation is stone pillars at the corners, every 4 m along the walls and on a 4 m grid inside, with beams spanning them under the floor, instead of stone under every floor tile: far less stone.

0.12.1: A mountain cabin's gables are boarded, like the ends of a real log cabin's roof: the logs stacked in the gable had nothing holding them.

0.12.0: Three new styles for the cold and the wet. Mountain cabin: stacked core-wood log walls on a stone footing, no corner posts, a steep 67° roof that sheds snow, and a fire pit under its own chimney at the far end from the door. Stilt cabin: the floor raised 1.5, 2.5 or 3.5 m above the ground or the water, on a deck along the entrance side with rails and an open way in, and a stair down that stops at the waterline. Stone keep: walls of stone in metre courses, arrow loops on the upper storeys, timber floors and stairs inside, and a walkable roof behind a parapet with merlons. The 67° roof pitch is offered for the timber hall and longhouse too.

0.11.3: Longhouse smoke holes are left open: the lower roof piece that sat over each one kept the smoke in.

0.11.2: No change you can see: the sketch drawing now lives in a library shared with BuildShapes (which draws its shapes the same way from BuildShapes 0.12.0).

0.11.1: Over a sketch, the markers and the outline's dashes are blue pencil instead of red chalk (red looked like an error).

0.11.0: The sketch draws itself in: new pieces appear bottom up, ink first, then their colour washes in from the bare paper. The whole hall when the outline becomes valid or the draft opens; only the added pieces when a setting adds some. Faint construction lines carry the floor, wall tops, eaves and ridge a little past the corners and gables. Roofs light as clean slopes instead of crumpled paper. Over a sketch, the corner, door and chimney markers and the outline's dashes are red chalk and the live line is ink, instead of glowing amber and white. hall status reports the drawing-in (sketch).

0.10.0: Sketch preview, now the default: the draft is drawn like a Piranesi watercolour. Pen strokes ink every edge where a piece folds or ends (running past the corners, bending a little, sometimes gone over twice) over a wash of the piece's own colour softened towards the paper, on watercolour-paper grain. It's solid and lit like the world around it; pieces that would fall are inked red on pink. Materials, Ghosts and Layout are still in the Preview row. Saved drafts open in Sketch.

0.9.0: Hallwright on its own, split out of BuildShapes (it was BuildShapes 0.8.10's Hallwright). Pick the Hallwright stake in the hammer's build menu (Misc): click corners, Shift+click entrances, Ctrl+click smoke holes or chimneys, E inside the outline (or L) for the settings, where the style row picks Timber hall, Longhouse or Stave temple. There's no F4 entry any more. Saved drafts and ground records are the same files, and the first start takes your Hallwright, key and shared-draft settings from BuildShapes' config. Update BuildShapes to 0.9.0 too: older BuildShapes still has its own stake. Live hall drafts are shared between Hallwright players.
