Norse building pieces for the hammer (Building tab, at a workbench).

Turf roofs: sod on birch bark over boards, in every shape the game's wooden roofs come in (26° and 45°: straight, ridge, outer and inner corner). Each one snaps, holds and wears exactly like the wooden roof it's shaped on, so the two mix freely. The grass is the game's own (from its unused turf roof) and sways in the wind. Open edges tear: the sod reaches out in lumps and droops over the edge, with sticks poking out of the soil. Sides that meet another roof piece stay clean, so a long roof shows no seams. A turf roof keeps the house warm: +1 comfort when you're sheltered under one (once, however many pieces). 2 wood and 1 fine wood (birch: its bark).

Smoke-hole roofs (26° and 45°), for single-slope roofs: a turf roof with a metre-square hole through it and a little hooded vent over the hole, as a longhouse without a chimney let out its hearth's smoke. Put it over the fire: the smoke rises up the hole and rolls out under the hood, and the hood keeps the rain off the fire below. 4 wood and 1 fine wood.

Turf ridge smoke vents (26° and 45°): a ridge piece with a slot open along its whole length and a single-pitch hood over it. A house's smoke gathers all along under its ridge, so put them in the ridge right over the hearth: the smoke rises out under the hood, the hood keeps the rain off the fire. Keep the slot clear (no ridge log over it). 4 wood and 1 fine wood.

Shingle roofs (26° and 45°, every shape): rows of wooden shingles with rounded tongues, tarred dark, as a stave church is roofed, each row half a shingle along from the one below and lapping over its heads, so a long roof reads as one. Ridges are capped with two boards, hips with a pair down the hip, valleys lined with a pair. They snap, hold and wear like the wooden roofs. 2 wood and 1 resin (the tar).

Shingle ridge with dragon head (26° and 45°): the end piece of a shingled ridge, with a carved dragon's head rising out of it and reaching out past the gable, jaws open, a crest down its neck: put it last on the ridge, the head facing out. 2 wood, 4 fine wood, 1 resin.

Stave church pieces: a stave wall (upright tarred planks standing in a sill under a wall plate; snaps and holds like the game's wooden wall; 4 wood, 1 resin), a stave post (a round tarred post with a carved band and a swelling capital, like the game's 2 m pole; 2 wood, 1 resin), and a carved portal door (the game's own door set in carved jambs wound with vines, a beast's head on each, and a carved arch over it standing proud of the wall above; 10 wood, 6 fine wood, 2 resin).

Reloads in a world. Native Linux and Windows.
