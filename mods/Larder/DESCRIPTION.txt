Larder: food that keeps. Preserved foods for long voyages, smoked, salted and dried; flatbread from a stone bake oven; the dairy: butter, skyr and cheese from the milk of Byre's goats and cows; and, if the server switches it on, spoilage. All at stations you build at a workbench (Crafting tab). Every station works while you're away, as the game's smelters do (up to an hour).

Smokehouse: a plank smoke hut over a stone firebox. Hang up meat (boar, deer or neck tail) and keep the fire fed with wood: a piece is smoked every 5 minutes, a log each, and it holds 10. Smoked meat: 36 health, 14 stamina, 50 minutes. 12 stone, 20 wood, 4 resin.

Salt pan: a shallow iron pan on hearth stones. Build it in the shallows, standing in the sea, and feed it firewood: a wood boils off a salt each minute, and it holds 20. Out of the water it waits (its hover says so). 12 stone, 2 bronze.

Salting barrel: pack meat (boar, deer or neck tail) in with a salt for each piece (the salt goes in as the barrel's fuel): a piece is cured every 10 minutes, and it holds 10. Salt meat: 40 health, 12 stamina, an hour. 12 wood, 6 bronze nails.

Drying rack: a rack of rough poles. Hang up raw fish (cut at the prep table from any fish you catch): out under the open sky each dries into stockfish in 20 minutes, the fish showing on the bars, 8 at a time (a full rack takes over 5 days). Under a roof it won't dry. Stockfish: 28 health, 26 stamina, an hour, and it weighs next to nothing. 12 wood.

The game's own smoked fish and smoked moose (Deep North, at the cauldron) are other, later foods: Larder's are the plainer ones you take on a voyage before then.

Bake oven: a stone and clay dome over a firebox. Mix flatbread dough at the cauldron (4 barley flour make 2), put it in the oven's mouth, and keep the fire fed with wood: it bakes in 50 seconds, four at a time (take it out when it's done, or it burns to coal, as in the game's oven). Flatbread: 20 health, 55 stamina, 40 minutes, light, and it keeps. Weaker than the game's oven bread, which is a meal of its own; this is what you carry. 20 stone, 6 wood.

Butter churn: a tall stave churn with a dasher. Churn 3 cow's milk into a pat of butter (14 health, 48 stamina, 30 minutes), or 2 goat's milk and a handful of blueberries into 2 bowls of skyr (25 health, 25 stamina, 25 minutes). 12 wood, 2 resin.

Cheese press: a cheese hoop under a weighted lever. Pour in milk, a goat's or a cow's (E), and leave it: each milk is pressed and set into a cheese in 4 minutes, and the press holds 20. Take the cheese out at the bench's end. Cheese is the keeping food: 32 health, 12 stamina, and it lasts 40 minutes, for a long voyage. It works while you're away, as the game's smelters do (up to an hour). 16 wood, 8 stone, 2 leather scraps.

Spoilage (a server setting, OFF unless the server turns it on): food goes off over game days unless it's preserved. Raw fish keeps a day, Byre's milk a day and a half, raw meat two, cooked food three, stews, soups and pies four, bread five, butter and cheese six, roots ten. Under a roof it goes a quarter slower; in a root cellar (a roofed room dug a metre or more into the ground, open on one side at most for the way down) about three times slower; in the Mountains or the Deep North slower again. Smoked meat, salt meat, stockfish, flatbread, the game's jerky, honey and jam, and eggs (they hatch) never spoil. A food's tooltip says how fresh it is. Spoiled food turns into as much "spoiled food" in its place, never lost silently. It's worked out when a chest is opened and, for your bag, once a minute: nothing runs over every chest in the world. SpoilSpeed sets how fast. Off, nothing changes at all.

The dairy needs Byre for the milk. Without it the churn and the press don't show in the hammer's menu (any already built stay standing), and nothing is lost.

Removing Larder: as with any mod that adds things, its foods, salt, dough, spoiled food and stations are gone for good once a character or a place is loaded without it (the game drops what it doesn't know from bags, chests and the ground, and takes down the pieces), and so is whatever is waiting in a station.
