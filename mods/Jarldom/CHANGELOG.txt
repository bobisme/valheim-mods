0.1.216: Homespun 0.2.0's dyed wool garments are as warm as undyed, and haulers leave Homespun's dye vat alone (it's a chest to the game, but not the stores).

0.1.215: clothes wear out. A tunic, dress, hat or Homespun garment frays with the hours worked in it (half again at heavy work: felling, mining, farming, hunting, building; and in the rain, and the cold; a hat half as fast): new to worn thin in about half a season of work. Worn thin, its wearer mends it at the sewing table (or the bench that made it, without Homespun) for 2 linen thread from the stores; twice, and the third time it wears through and is gone, and they're in rags till the weaver's next garment. Below a quarter it's threadbare (-3). The smith leaves clothes alone. A garment lasts about a season and a half in all, so a colony keeps a weaver busy. Live-checked: a tunic mended at the sewing table (2 thread taken, the mending counted, kept over a relog), one mended twice wearing through at work, both wearers dressed again from the stores.

0.1.214: the jarldom stations test command says what each station would be fed and whether it's left alone, and for a textile station whether it's under a roof and what it takes. Live-checked with 0.1.213's cloth for the cold: a weaver spinning wool, weaving bolts and sewing wool trousers in winter, which someone took to wear.

0.1.213: wool for the cold. Winter out of doors is cold, and the Mountains and the far north are cold in any season (a fire's warmth never is): a villager there with nothing warm on is glum (Cold, -6, for a few hours after). Warm is Homespun's wool tunic, dress, trousers or hooded cloak, or a cape that keeps out frost (wolf, lox). In autumn and winter villagers fetch something warm from the stores and wear a warm cape over a sturdier one; everyone going out with the jarl packs something warm; with Homespun the weaver sews wool trousers (16 wool) and hooded cloaks (25) for those who have none. The colony window says how many have nothing warm and where wool comes from. Live-checked in winter: the Cold moodlet on villagers out of doors, a wolf cape put in the stores fetched and worn, the colony window's line. Not yet seen: a cloak sewn (this colony has no wool yet), an expedition packing one, the mountains.

0.1.212: farmers learn a crop a mod adds after the colony first looked, or a crop mod reloaded: the cultivator's crops were read once a game, so Homespun's nettle (and any crop of a mod reloaded mid-game) never reached the fields' choices. Live-checked: nettle in a field's crops after Homespun was reloaded while Jarldom ran.

0.1.211: with Homespun 0.1.4, nettle is a fibre crop as flax is. Farmers sow it in the fields (it grows in the Meadows and the Black Forest) and foragers pick it wild; it's stored as seed, and ten are kept back for sowing as with flax. The weaver soaks it in the retting pool, breaks it at the flax brake and spins line nettle on the wheel, or by hand at two line nettle to two thread where line flax gives two from one. 0.1.209's rule now covers both: flax and nettle are sown and replanted only while a weaver is short of fibre (fewer than 30 of the two together), flax before nettle where both grow, and food otherwise. Without Homespun nothing changes. Live-checked: loads with Homespun 0.1.4, the weaver's stock list counts nettle. Not yet seen in play: nettle sown, picked or worked by villagers (this colony has thousands of flax, so flax always comes first).

0.1.210: herders shear and milk Byre's animals, and a byre warms the longhouse beside it. With Byre 0.2.0, a herder shears a sheep in full fleece and milks a goat whose milk is in, in a pasture, with shears or a milk pail from the stores, and takes the wool and milk home (the pasture's idle line says when the stores lack them). A new Byre room (roofed, with Byre's trough or hay rack; points for feeders, stall dividers and fence gates) gives +1 comfort to a longhouse sharing at least 2 m of wall with it. Fence gates count toward a byre's points. Live-checked: a herder milked a goat and sheared a sheep and the milk and wool reached the stores; a byre built against a longhouse graded C and raised it from 11 to 13 points. Not yet seen live: the fence gate count.

0.1.209: fields grow food, not flax nobody wants. Farmers sowed whichever seed the stores held most of, and replanted each harvest with the same crop: with thousands of flax stored, every field went to flax and the colony starved. Flax is now sown and replanted only while a weaver is short of it (or chosen for the season in the zone's window); otherwise a food crop, those eaten as picked (carrots) before those the cook needs (turnips).

0.1.208: a villager in a fight takes up the bow or a weapon whatever they were at. Two hunters met a neck on the way to the hall and swung the hall's tankard at it all night, starving and sleepless.

0.1.207: legends no longer crowd the map. At most four legend pins at once on a jarl's map (the newest kept; a map from before is thinned once), a pin comes off once the jarl has stood at the place, and no new legend is told in the hall while four are pinned and not yet found (one told ten days ago no longer counts).

0.1.206: with Homespun, the wool chain works through: lox pelts combed at the flax brake (two always kept, and only as many as the yarn wants), wool spun by hand or at the spinning wheel (Byre's fleece too), woven into bolts, a band, and a wool tunic a villager put on. A station is fed the fibre the stores are shorter of first, so a brake with flax to break still combs a pelt. The wheel spins line flax and wool while thread or yarn is short (waiting line flax no longer counts as thread). A spinning wheel out in the open (the game's wheel spins only under a roof) is neither fed nor counted on, so the weaver spins by hand instead. Stations are emptied a few at a time, not one thread a trip. Villagers bathing, or with clothes in the bag to put on, no longer count as in rags (the weaver made clothes for the bathhouse). Test commands: jarldom strip <name>; jarldom weave shows the stores and the weaver.

0.1.205: with Homespun, the weaver works its chain: soaks flax in the retting pool (never the seed flax), breaks it at the flax brake, takes out what the stations hold, spins line flax at the spinning wheel or by hand, weaves bolts at the loom and sews at the sewing table: Homespun's wool tunic or dress, Hildir's dyed clothes from linen, or the plain undertunic, whichever can be made, a step at a time. The stations rest while the stores hold plenty of thread or yarn; a lox pelt is combed only while yarn runs short. Homespun's garments count as clothes. Live-checked: flax to a yellow tunic with beads on a villager who was in rags. Not yet: the wool chain, a spinning wheel. Test command: jarldom weave.
