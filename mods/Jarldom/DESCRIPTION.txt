A colony of the reborn, built on Valheim's own systems. Raise the High-seat pillars, take in the fallen Odin sends, give them beds, work and gear, keep them fed and happy, defend them, and send the worthy to Valhalla on a funeral pyre.

Founding: High-seat pillars (hammer, Misc; 2 fine wood, 4 wood, 1 resin, near a workbench) found a colony 60 m across (more as it grows); colonies stand 300 m apart. Use the pillars to hold council: the colony window shows the hall, its people, rooms, work, the Thing, the gods and the saga. A new colony's hall tab lists the first steps in order, ticking them off, and the pillars' hover says what's next.

Odin's attention (renown) is wealth (what you've built, coins and valuables in its chests), comfort (the beds' comfort, the game's own) and deeds (raids held, feasts, souls sent to Valhalla). Once renown reaches 20 (25 for the second, 31 for the third, then more gently: 83 at the tenth, 283 at the thirtieth) and a bed is free, Odin may send someone at dawn: Hugin says who comes, and at dusk a Valkyrie sets them down at the pillars. Take them in or turn them away from the colony window. Each soul has an Old Norse name, two traits, a profession they'd like and the story of how they fell; souls who fell together arrive together and like each other.

Villagers live as players do: they eat (three meals, from their bag or the colony's chests; health follows food), sleep in their own bed (or by the hall fire, unhappily), go to the great hall at dusk to sit, eat and talk, and shelter from heavy rain. They fight with what you give them (melee, shields, bows), flee when hurt, and when they fall they rise again at their bed with their own gear, then walk back to their tombstone for the rest. One with no bed, or disgraced, is lost for good and rises as a named draugr carrying their hoard.

Professions: hauler (carries to the stockpiles and keeps the fires, torches and braziers fed; anyone idle lends a hand), woodcutter (fells, clears stumps, plants saplings; stops when the stores hold enough wood, which the Work tab sets), forager, farmer (hoes, sows, harvests and replants with seeds from the stores), guard, miner, hunter, fisherman, smelter (feeds smelters and kilns through their own controls, fuel first), cook (cooks, brews mead bases at the mead ketill, keeps fermenters going), smith (makes what you commission from the Work tab), healer, scout (fills in the map, reports what's out there), herder (feeds a pasture's tame beasts, culls the surplus), weaver (spins flax into linen and weaves and dyes tunics, dresses and caps, so no one goes in rags), skald and more. Work comes from a job board: zones you mark (woodlot, quarry, fields, foraging and hunting grounds, fishing dock), watch posts you raise for the guards' rounds, things lying in the colony, fires and stations running low. Jobs are leases, so two villagers (or two players' games) never take the same one; the board says why a job is blocked ("no pickaxe of tier 2"). Gear matters: an axe's tier, durability that wears. Villagers fetch the tools of their trade (and arrows) from the stockpiles, and an idle villager says what's missing.

Rooms: with the hammer's "Mark rooms and zones" chosen, click a room's corners on the floor, click the first again, and choose what it is. Click one spot twice for a round work zone; E on a zone makes it wider or narrower. Work zones have soft edges: a log that rolls out of the woodlot is still cut up. Rooms are checked the game's way (walls round the edge, roof over the floor, a door, what's inside) and graded D to S; the overlay shows rooms, zones and the gaps in walls. Longhouse, great hall, stockpile, smithy, Odin's shrine, temples, healer's house, treasure room, burial ground, watchtower. Ranks from camp to hold need people and rooms, and raise the colony's cap and radius. Houses of storeys work: each storey is its own room, and villagers climb the stairs to beds upstairs.

The colony keeps to its own ground: villagers take beds only in its rooms or by the pillars, and pick up what lies about only in its rooms, zones and by the pillars, so a neighbour's base inside the radius is left alone. They walk through players and each other.

Mood: seven needs (food, rest, shelter, company, safety, faith, beauty) and moodlets (a fine meal, a feast, the hall, an own bed, unpaid wages, a friend lost...) make a mood from -100 to +100 that sets how fast they work (60% to 130%); the panel says why. Miserable villagers slack, brawl and steal, and the disgraced may desert.

The gold goes somewhere: wages every five days from the treasury chest (all or nothing; unpaid villagers grumble), feasts in the great hall, offerings at the gods' altars and coins thrown into a sacred bog for favour without end. Gilded high seat and banners make halls grand. The Thing is held when you call it: matters drawn from what really happened (beds short, wages owed, a thief, a feud) are raised day by day, each by someone it touches, and wait; call the Thing and everyone gathers in the great hall, each petitioner comes before the high seat and speaks, and you rule on each there (E on the one who spoke if you put it off). Matters seen to are withdrawn; left unheard five days, they sour the colony. Worthy villagers (100 worth from kills, work and feasts) can be sent to Valhalla on a funeral pyre at dusk with grave goods: deeds, Odin's favour and a guaranteed newcomer.

Raids at a colony sound the war horn: fighters arm themselves and go out to the raiders, the rest run for the great hall. Raids grow with renown. The Fulings send a herald to a hamlet or more: pay by dusk (more each time), or they raid; paying mid-raid turns them back.

Visitors and threats: a master teaches at village rank, Dvergr mercenaries can be hired at jarldom rank after the Queen, captives taken by the Fulings wait three days at a Fuling camp for rescue or ransom, and after Bonemass a draugr jarl's barrow sends warbands until he falls. The gothi keeps the temples; Thor, Freyja and the other gods bless those who keep them; a feast with a temple is a festival. Skalds play a drum (workbench). One newcomer in twelve, after the first boss, may be Forsaken-touched: find them by their mischief and cast them out at the Thing. While you were away: an unloaded colony makes up the time when you return.

Moving the pillars: taken down or broken, they leave the heart of the high seat; raise new pillars while carrying it and the colony stands there again. E on a marked room or zone (marker out) to check, change or remove it. Works with Omens (Odin's favour is the gods' Fate), AICompanion (claims respected both ways), Gary (villagers pet him), SkalTavern; needs none of them.

Install on the host and every player: a host without Jarldom deletes the pillars and villagers when their area loads (the hammer refuses Jarldom pieces where the host lacks it). Before uninstalling, disband the colony from its window: everyone's gear goes into chests by the pillars. Claude Tools adds the jarldom command (status, roster, villager, rooms, board, log, and test commands for single player and hosts).

Clothes: everyone is reborn in rags. A weaver spins flax (which grows in the colony's fields whatever the biome; a trader brings the first seed) into linen thread, by hand or at a spinning wheel, and at the workbench weaves tunics and dresses dyed with blueberries, raspberries, dandelions or bark, with shawls, capes or amber beads, and fur caps from hides. Ore lies out in the wilds, so miners work claims: drive an ore claim (hammer → Misc) in beside a deposit you've found (copper, tin, silver; by a sunken crypt's door for its iron, once the Elder is beaten) and the colony's miners go out to it and come back with the ore, more with a cart at home. Build a camp round the stake (a bed, a chest and a fire) and they work everything near it, as many at once as it has beds, staying the day. Each trip is told, not walked (the world far from a player stands still): the stake surveys what's there whenever its ground is loaded, and deposits the miners worked out are gone when you next come by. The wilds are dangerous: a miner may come back wounded, or carried home without the ore and their pickaxe; a camp makes it safer, and an idle guard goes along. Once Hildir is known, traders spend some of a run's coins at her stall for anyone still in rags. Trade runs grow with the colony: from village rank a trader carries what the stores have to sell (amber, silver, gems and the like) on foot, or takes a cart standing in the colony for a bigger load; at jarldom rank a karve moored by the colony sails to better-paying markets down the coast, and at hold rank a longship or drakkar to the far ones. They take the smallest way that carries what there is; the sea pays better but Njord decides who comes home. The Work tab says how the next run would go and what the next way needs. Villagers fetch clothes from the stores and are the happier for them. The clothes are the game's own; the recipes are the colony's, so a player's crafting is unchanged.

Time off: a villager with no work plays hnefatafl at a hall table, wrestles (glíma) with whoever will take them on, spars at a training dummy, shoots at an archery target, lifts stones or takes a walk; otherwise they pass the time in the great hall. Why they're idle still shows in the colony window.

Out with the jarl: from a villager's panel, Follow me takes them along on your travels. They keep up, through portals too, fight what comes at you, and when they come home the saga tells of it. Go home sends them back.

Doors: a villager shuts a door behind them if it was shut, and leaves an open door open.
