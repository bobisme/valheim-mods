A colony of the reborn, built on Valheim's own systems. Raise the high-seat pillars, take in the fallen Odin sends, give them beds, work and gear, keep them fed and happy, defend them, hold the Thing, and send the worthy to Valhalla on a funeral pyre.

Getting started:
1. Build the High-seat pillars (hammer, Misc: 6 wood, 2 resin, near a workbench) in open ground, runes to the front. They found a colony; E on them (or between them) opens the colony window. Name your hall there. They start as two lashed logs and are raised anew at each rank, up to gilded carvings lit by fire at a hold.
2. Build a bed. Odin sends the first villager once the colony's renown reaches 20 and a bed is free: Hugin tells you at dawn, and a Valkyrie sets them down at the pillars at dusk. Take them in from the colony window.
3. Mark rooms and work zones with the hammer's "Mark rooms and zones" piece: Ctrl+Shift+click on a room's floor and it's found from its walls; click a spot twice for a round work zone (woodlot, quarry, fields, foraging or hunting ground, fishing dock). Give each villager a trade from their panel (E on them).
4. Put chests in a marked stockpile room. To choose what one holds, or make it the treasury for wages, look at it with the room marker out and press Shift+E.
5. Raise a great hall (walls, roof, a door, a hearth, seats and tables) and mark it: villagers gather there at dusk, and the Thing is held there.
The hall tab of the colony window lists these first steps and ticks them off, and says what needs you.

Renown and newcomers: Odin's attention is the colony's wealth (what you've built, coins and valuables in its chests), comfort (the beds' comfort, the game's own) and deeds (raids held, feasts, souls sent to Valhalla). Each newcomer needs a little more (20, 25, 31, ... 83 at the tenth). Each soul has an Old Norse name, two traits, a trade they'd like and the story of how they fell; souls who fell together arrive together. Ranks from camp to hold need people and rooms, and raise the colony's size and how many may live there.

Villagers live as players do: they eat (three meals, from their bag or the stores; health follows food), sleep in their own bed, gather in the great hall at dusk, and shelter from storms with games and talk. They fight with what you give them (melee, shields, bows), flee when hurt, and when they fall they rise again at their bed with their own gear, then walk back to their tombstone for the rest. One with no bed rises by the pillars, slower; one who dies disgraced is lost for good and rises as a named draugr carrying their hoard. They open doors, and shut those they found shut. Hover over a villager to see what they're doing; E for their panel: mood and why, needs, skills, gear, bed and work.

Work: hauler (to the stockpiles, and keeps the fires, torches and braziers fed), woodcutter, forager, farmer (sows, harvests and replants), guard (walks the watch posts you raise), miner, hunter, fisherman, smelter, cook (cleans the catch at a food preparation table and cooks it), smith (makes what you commission), healer, scout, herder, carter, trader, weaver, gothi, skald. Jobs come from a job board: the zones you mark, things lying about, fires and stations running low. The Work tab shows it, says why a job is blocked ("needs a pickaxe of tier 2"), and sets how much wood, stone and ore the stores should keep. Villagers fetch the tools of their trade from the stores, make their own bows, arrows and tankards at the workbench, and say what's missing when they can't work. Builders build BuildOrders' plans, mend what's damaged, rebuild what raiders broke, and pave the paths villagers wear. An idle villager plays hnefatafl, wrestles, spars at a training dummy, shoots at an archery target, lifts stones or takes a walk.

Rooms: rooms are checked the game's way (walls round the edge, a roof, a door, what's inside) and graded D to S: longhouse, great hall, stockpile, smithy, Odin's shrine, temples, healer's house, brewhouse, treasure room, burial ground, watchtower. Work zones have soft edges, and E on a zone's marker makes it wider or narrower. Houses of storeys work: villagers climb the stairs to beds upstairs. The colony keeps to its own ground: a neighbour's base inside its circle is left alone.

Mood: seven needs (food, rest, shelter, company, safety, faith, beauty) and moodlets (a fine meal, a feast, the hall, clothes, unpaid wages, a friend lost...) make a mood from -100 to +100 that sets how fast they work. Miserable villagers slack, brawl and steal, and the disgraced may desert. A disgraced villager can win their name back: do the deed they ask of you, or let them fight in a raid the colony holds.

Requests: now and then a villager asks something of the jarl, chosen from who they are (traits, backstory, a friend's killer) and only ever what your world's progress can reach: a trophy, goods from a land, a relic from the old dead, a starred beast killed with your own hands, or a far place seen. It shows on their hover, their panel and the hall tab, and lapses after ten days without harm. Take it on from their panel (a place goes on your map), then hand it over or tell of it: they think better of you, gain worth, pay you from their own hoard, and the colony gains renown.

The gold goes somewhere: wages every five days from the treasury (all or nothing), feasts in the great hall, offerings at the gods' altars (each god blesses those who keep a temple), coins thrown into a sacred bog for Odin's favour, a gilded high seat and banners. Worthy villagers can be sent to Valhalla on a funeral pyre with grave goods: deeds, Odin's favour and a newcomer.

The Thing: matters drawn from what really happens (beds short, wages owed, a thief, a feud) are raised day by day, each by someone it touches. Call the Thing from the colony window: everyone gathers in the great hall, each petitioner comes before the high seat and speaks, and you rule on each from the bar at the foot of the screen. Promises made there are remembered, kept or broken; matters left unheard five days sour the colony.

Raids and threats: raids sound the war horn; fighters go out to the raiders and the rest run for the great hall. Raids grow with renown. The Fulings send a herald: pay by dusk or they raid. Captives wait three days at a Fuling camp for rescue or ransom; after Bonemass a draugr jarl's barrow sends warbands until he falls; one newcomer in twelve may be Forsaken-touched, to be found by their mischief and cast out at the Thing. A master may come to teach, and Dvergr mercenaries can be hired after the Queen.

Clothes: everyone is reborn in rags. A weaver spins flax (a trader brings the first seed; it grows in the colony's fields) into linen and weaves tunics, dresses and caps dyed with berries and bark. Once Hildir is known, traders buy clothes at her stall.

Ore claims: ore lies out in the wilds. Drive an ore claim (hammer, Misc) in beside a deposit you've found (copper, tin, silver; at a sunken crypt's door for its iron, once the Elder is beaten), and miners go out to it and come home with what one can carry (about 25 ore), more with the colony's cart where a cart can go. Build a camp round the stake (a bed, a chest and a fire) and they work everything near it, one miner a bed. They go for the ore the stores lack most, and stop when the stores hold enough. The wilds are dangerous: a miner may come back wounded, or without the ore and their pickaxe; a camp and a guard along make it safer. Trips are told, not walked (the world far from a player stands still); deposits they worked out are gone when you next pass by.

Trade runs: from village rank a trader takes what the stores have to sell (amber, silver, gems) on foot, or with a cart in the colony; at jarldom rank a karve moored by the colony sails to better markets, at hold rank a longship or drakkar to the far ones. The Work tab says how the next run would go.

Out with the jarl: Follow me on a villager's panel takes them along on your travels, through portals too; they fight what comes at you, and Go home sends them back. The Expedition tab sets out with a crew, each with a role (guard, cook, scout, carrier) and packing for it; E on one of them makes camp where you stand (a fire, a chest, a workbench). With the Camping mod the crew also lays bedrolls round the fire and a lean-to over yours (a tent instead in the mountains), and sleeps on them at night.

Great works: once it's a village, the Thing may ask to raise a great work, one at a time, with BuildOrders laying out its plan for the builders. The Lighthouse by the shore: its fire is the colony's beacon and trade ships are lost half as often. The Peak Temple to the best-pleased god, an A-frame hof for the snow line, doubles that god's blessing and offerings: on the colony's highest ground, or on the highest peak within 1.5 km (pinned on your map). For the peak, set out with a crew with a builder and carriers (they pack its makings from the stores), make camp near the summit, and E on the builder to raise it there; the builder works from the camp and the carriers' packs, and tells you what to bring when the camp chest runs short.

Ships: mark a Boatyard zone out over the shore (with a workbench by it, as for any ship), make someone a Shipwright (they need a hammer), and order a raft, karve or longship in the Work tab. The shipwright fetches its makings from the stores and works on it at the water's edge a good while, and the colony cheers it into the water. Trade runs go by karve or longship once one lies at the shore. One ship at a time; you must know how to build that ship yourself.

While you're away: an unloaded colony makes up the time when you return. Outposts: a second colony 300 m or more away can be linked to the first, and a carter brings its ore home.

Moving the pillars: taken down or broken, they leave the heart of the high seat; raise new pillars while carrying it and the colony stands there again.

Running it together: the founder lets other players run the colony from the hall tab (only the founder chooses); they mark rooms, set trades, take newcomers in and call the Thing.

Installing and multiplayer: install on every player and on the server. A server without Jarldom deletes the pillars and villagers when their area loads (the hammer refuses Jarldom pieces there). Everyone on a server should run the same version: games tell each other theirs, and while two differ in what they save, no colony is saved and everyone is told who has which.

Uninstalling: disband the colony from its window first: everyone's gear goes into chests by the pillars. Without Jarldom the game removes its pillars and villagers.

Works with Omens (Odin's favour is the gods' Fate), AICompanion (beds respected both ways), BuildOrders (builders build its plans), Gary (villagers pet him), SkalTavern (villagers drink its drinks and answer your Skål!); needs none of them. Claude Tools adds the jarldom command (status, roster, villager, rooms, board, log, and test commands for single player and hosts).
