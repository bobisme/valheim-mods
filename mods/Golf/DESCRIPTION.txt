Meadow Golf: build a little Viking golf course out of the landscape and your own structures. Craft a carved wooden golf club at a workbench (6 wood, 2 leather scraps). Hammer → Furniture supplies golf tees (2 wood) and cups with linen flags (4 wood, 1 stone).

Shift+E labels a tee or cup as Course:Hole:Par, for example Meadow:1:3. Give both ends the same course name and hole number; the tee sets par. The builder can edit labels. Courses have up to 18 holes, par 2–8; use a unique number for each cup. A matching cup must be within 240 m when you start; the host can find its saved marker even outside your loaded area.

Equip the club and E at a tee to set your colored wooden ball. Stand within 3 m of the stationary ball, aim with the camera, use the wheel to choose Drive / Chip / Putt, then hold left mouse to charge and release to swing. Gentle putts begin at low power; charging takes 1.4 seconds. An amber path and stopping-point ring predict flight, bounces and rolling with the actual ball physics. The HUD distinguishes predicted range (with its sampled power) from distance to the cup. Right click cancels charging. The native swing contact event launches the ball, with a wooden impact and quieter bounce sounds. Prediction is spread across frames; unreliable results show a short direction line. Balls roll, bounce and hit real terrain and buildings; they do not damage anything. Players and creatures pass through balls, and golfers cannot hit someone else's ball. A slow ball entering its linked cup finishes the hole.

G opens the match and scorecard window using Valheim's own wooden inventory panel, recessed score area and fonts. Stand within 5 m of hole 1: [1] starts a 9-hole match, [2] starts 18 holes, and [J] joins the open match. The host checks all saved numbered tees and cups, even outside loaded areas, and names missing/duplicate endpoints or holes longer than 240 m. Each course name identifies one course: use distinct names for separate courses. A front nine may also be played on an 18-hole course. Everyone has a separate painted ball and saved card; a shared match ID groups the results. E at each next tee continues after you finish the current hole. Match play prevents skipping unfinished holes or replaying completed holes. Finishing hole 9 or 18 completes your round. [X] stops your own round and preserves its finished scores; [M] ends the shared match, allowed for its starter, the first tee's builder or the local server host. Ending hides/freezes the balls and keeps the results. [R] refreshes the host scoreboard, including participants whose balls are outside loaded areas; up to eight rows are displayed. G/Escape closes the window. New rounds replace your previous card; stopped participants cannot restart within the same match. End that match and start another for a fresh round.

The landscape matters: uncleared grass has more rolling resistance than cleared ground or built floors; Black Forest/Mistlands rough is heavier, swamp mud is heaviest, and mountain/Deep North snow slows rolls too. Clear a fairway with a hoe, build banked paths or bridges, and use real trees, rocks, walls, ramps and ceilings as obstacles. Gravity and actual colliders drive rebounds and downhill rolls. Entering native water or liquid hazards automatically returns the ball to the last shot's lie for exactly one extra stroke. A flooded tee is rejected. The preview uses the same surface rules and marks a predicted water entry in blue with a +1 warning. Preview liquid levels exclude momentary waves for stable course rules. There are no terrain changes, weather forces or damage to trees/buildings.

For casual practice, E at any tee starts a hole and Ctrl+E starts a fresh practice card, leaving your current match card. Practice replays replace that hole's result; changing courses starts a new card. E on your ball returns it to the last shot's lie for one penalty; E on the same unfinished hole's tee returns it to the tee for one penalty. There is a 99-stroke ceiling. Cup rings are visual and never excavate terrain. Indoor and basement courses use actual floors, rather than lifting the ball to outdoor terrain height.

Install Meadow Golf on the host and every player, then restart before loading a world: the club, tees, cups and balls are new saved prefabs. Mouse/keyboard controls; G is adjustable in F7. No dependency on BuildOrders or Gary. Reloads cancel a charged swing and reattach Golf's scripts to saved pieces/balls. No periodic whole-scene scans; the host indexes saved golf balls once per world/reload in batches.

Compiled on native Linux with rule tests and exact installed-game API checks. See VALIDATION.md for the current Creative test results and remaining remote multiplayer qualification. Optional ClaudeTools commands include golf status, golf match 9|18|join|end|stop|scores, and golf shot drive|chip|putt <power> <dx> <dz>. Disposable roundtest and fixture commands are limited to locally hosted Creative; they do not terraform or modify existing buildings.
